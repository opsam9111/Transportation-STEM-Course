Welcome to the Course

Welcome to Teaching Smart, Safe, and Sustainable Transportation. This course will help you understand foundational transportation STEM concepts and translate them into learning experiences that fit the subjects, learners, resources, and communities you already serve.

You do not need advanced transportation, science, or programming experience. The course begins with everyday transportation systems and gradually introduces energy, sensing, data, algorithms, intelligent transportation, accessibility, equity, electric mobility, and classroom design.

Your role: Bring your professional experience, ask questions, test ideas, support classmates, and consider how each concept could work in your context.

Course Content and Structure

The semester includes an orientation, seven two-week units, and a final showcase. Plan for approximately two hours per week.

  1. Transportation Systems, Communities, and Careers
  2. Motion, Energy, and Sustainable Transportation
  3. Sensors, Data, and Real-World Conditions
  4. Algorithms, Communication, and Intelligent Transportation
  5. Safety, Accessibility, and Equity
  6. Electric Mobility and Charging Communities
  7. Designing for Classroom Transfer
  8. Final Showcase and Reflection

Every unit follows five steps: Learn, Experience, Adapt, Apply, and Reflect.

How to Succeed

  • Schedule two regular study periods each week rather than waiting for one long period.
  • Begin with the unit overview and objectives.
  • Use the low-bandwidth, printable, simulated, or unplugged alternative when equipment is unavailable.
  • Ask questions early through email or the discussion forum.
  • Connect each concept with a subject or community example you already know.
  • Save useful ideas in your classroom-transfer plan as you move through the course.
  • Respond respectfully to classmates and learn from contexts different from your own.
  • Use feedback to revise rather than treating the first submission as the final word.

Assessment and Feedback

  • Knowledge checks: 15%
  • Discussion contributions: 15%
  • Activity reflections and applications: 25%
  • Classroom-transfer tasks: 20%
  • Final learning experience or implementation evidence: 25%

Knowledge checks permit multiple attempts. A score of 80% demonstrates mastery, and the highest score is recorded. Larger tasks use common success criteria even when participants choose different submission formats.

Late Work and Make-Up Policy

I understand that educators balance professional, family, and community responsibilities. Late work will normally be accepted without penalty when you communicate promptly about the situation. If illness, employment responsibilities, family needs, internet problems, electricity interruptions, or other circumstances affect your participation, contact me as soon as possible so that we can agree on a reasonable plan.

Collaborative deadlines remain important because classmates may depend on one another. Notify both your group and the instructor when you anticipate a delay. Missed quizzes and individual assignments will be managed according to the circumstances.

Responsible AI Use

You may use generative AI for brainstorming, concept clarification, example generation, grammar and language support, practice questions, lesson adaptation, early visual development, accessibility review, and feedback on a draft.

You remain responsible for the accuracy, appropriateness, and integrity of your submission. Review and correct AI output, protect personal and institutional information, and disclose meaningful assistance.

Suggested disclosure: I used [tool] to assist with [specific purpose]. I reviewed, corrected, and revised the output and take responsibility for the final submission.

Do not enter personally identifiable student information, confidential student work, private school or employer information, organizational records, or protected research materials into an AI tool.
