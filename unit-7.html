Unit 7: Designing for Classroom Transfer

Create, peer-review, and revise a classroom-ready transportation STEM experience.

Duration
Four hours across two weeks
Materials
Paper and pencil; browser optional
Evidence
Complete lesson plan, standards-evidence crosswalk, feedback notes, and revision rationale.
Unit sequenceObjectives1 Learn2 Experience3 Adapt4 Apply5 ReflectKnowledge checkFinish and continue

Learning objectives

  1. Align one measurable learning objective with an activity and assessment.
  2. Plan a feasible sequence of learning steps and subject-specific connections.
  3. Provide accessible participation and low-cost alternatives.
  4. Use peer feedback to revise a classroom-ready lesson.

Step 1 · Learn

Understand the concept

A compelling demonstration is not automatically a complete lesson. Classroom transfer requires a clear learning goal, a sequence that elicits learner thinking, suitable evidence of understanding, and practical alternatives when technology is unavailable.

Guiding question: Create, peer-review, and revise a classroom-ready transportation STEM experience.

Estimated learning time: 35 minutes. Read the explanation, create a brief concept sketch, and identify one transportation example in your community.

Step 2 · Experience

Model investigation: Turn investigations into teachable lessons

Allow 60–75 minutes. Work with a partner where possible. Record your reasoning, not just the final answer.

  1. Choose one transportation concept from Units 1–6 and identify grade, subject, and community context.
  2. Write one measurable objective using an observable verb and specify what evidence will demonstrate it.
  3. Plan a 35–50 minute sequence: engage with a question; investigate; explain with evidence; apply; check understanding.
  4. List required materials, safety procedures, accessibility supports, and an unplugged pathway.
  5. Connect to a specific, verified subject standard and state exactly how the assessment demonstrates it.
  6. Exchange the draft with a colleague; collect one strength, one question, and one actionable improvement.
  7. Revise the lesson and explain what changed because of the feedback.

Evidence to keep: Complete lesson plan, standards-evidence crosswalk, feedback notes, and revision rationale.

Technology and unplugged pathways

With technology: Use a shared document, spreadsheet, digital diagram, or classroom presentation to collect and explain evidence. Without technology: Use printed scenario cards, graph paper, drawings, and group discussion to demonstrate the same objective.

Accessibility: Provide written and spoken directions, readable diagrams with text labels, flexible response formats, and group roles. Do not rely only on color or on personal student disclosures.

Standards connection

Select the precise NGSS or 2023 Alabama Course of Study expectation matching the chosen lesson; do not claim universal alignment. These are suggested connections; check the exact performance expectation and your assessment evidence before claiming full standards alignment.

Stage 6 · Teaching and assessment guide

Make the investigation classroom-ready

Teacher implementation sequence

  1. Introduce (5–10 minutes): Present a real community transportation situation and ask students to make an initial prediction.
  2. Define (10 minutes): State the challenge, criteria, constraints, and the evidence students will collect.
  3. Investigate (20–30 minutes): Complete the classroom lesson prototype in pairs; record observations in a labeled table or diagram.
  4. Analyze (15 minutes): Compare results, discuss alternative explanations, and identify limitations of the model.
  5. Improve (15 minutes): Revise a decision or design using evidence, and explain the expected benefit.
  6. Assess (5–10 minutes): Collect an exit ticket that includes a claim, supporting evidence, and one remaining question.

Evidence of learning

Pilot two versions or review two peer solutions, analyze feedback, and document a justified revision.

Subject and grade adaptations

Grade 6: adapt a map investigation. Grade 7: adapt a data-collection investigation. Grade 8: adapt an algorithm or design optimization investigation.

Standards evidence check

Suggested NGSS connection: MS-ETS1-3. This is a planning connection, not an assertion of complete alignment. Check that the actual student task and rubric assess the expectation. Alabama course-of-study codes must be confirmed against the subject and grade before adding a specific code.

Step 4 · Apply

Classroom transfer plan

  1. Identify the target grade and subject, and state one measurable learning objective.
  2. Choose a local transportation problem and activate prior knowledge with a prediction.
  3. Model the investigation and specify what students record or produce.
  4. Ask learners to interpret the evidence, compare alternatives, and explain a decision.
  5. Add a low-cost pathway and at least two accessibility supports.
  6. Define an exit ticket or performance task that directly measures the objective.

Submission: A one- to two-page classroom adaptation plan and the evidence from your investigation. Submit through your course LMS or the method provided by the instructor; this public website does not transmit assignments.
