Weeks 3 and 4

Motion Energy and Sustainable Transportation

Moving people and goods requires energy. Connect force, motion, energy transfer, batteries, solar energy, efficiency, and design choices with transportation.

Estimated time
Four hours across two weeks
Required technology
Browser; common-material alternatives
Major evidence
Energy-flow investigation
Unit sequenceObjectives1 Learn2 Experience3 Adapt4 Apply5 ReflectKnowledge checkSources

Learning Objectives

  1. Trace energy through a transportation system.
  2. Explain how load, terrain, and driving conditions influence energy demand.
  3. Compare energy pathways without treating any technology as impact-free.
  4. Design a low-cost investigation that preserves the scientific concept.
  5. Connect sustainable transportation with a local problem and career.

Step 1

Learn the Concept

Stored chemical energy→Electrical energy→Motor rotation→Vehicle motion+Heat and sound

An all-electric vehicle stores energy in a battery and uses one or more electric motors for propulsion. Regenerative braking can recover some energy, but no real system transfers energy with perfect efficiency.

Scientific caution: “No tailpipe emissions” does not mean “no environmental impact.” A complete comparison considers electricity generation, materials, manufacturing, infrastructure, use, and end-of-life decisions.

Step 2

Experience Relative Energy Demand

Explore how operating conditions may change energy demand. This simplified score supports comparison; it does not predict actual energy use or range.

Case from pedestrian-safety outreach

Learners built a pedestrian signal using a battery, light, connectors, and switch. They considered a traveler who could not rely on light alone and added sound. When asked for another energy source, they proposed a solar panel and used sunlight to power the circuit. The learning came from connecting energy source, circuit, output, accessibility, and a transportation need—not from the kit alone.

Step 3

Adapt for Different Resources

Standard materials

Use a small motorized vehicle or solar kit and measure one outcome.

Low cost

Use toy cars, ramps, added masses, rubber bands, balloons, or paper wheels.

Data pathway

Analyze a prepared table or graph and justify a design recommendation.

Describe visual changes verbally, provide labeled diagrams and tables, use tactile models where useful, and allow written, oral, visual, or recorded explanations under the same success criteria.

Step 4

Apply to Your Teaching

Design a short investigation around: How can we use stored or renewable energy to move people or goods more sustainably in our community?

  1. Select the grade, subject, and transportation context.
  2. Write one measurable objective.
  3. Identify energy input, transfers, useful output, and losses.
  4. Vary one condition and observe or measure one outcome.
  5. Provide a standard, low-cost, or data-based route.
  6. Add accessibility, a relevant career, and evidence of understanding.

Step 5

Reflect and Revise

Knowledge Check

1. Which statement best describes energy in an electric vehicle?
2. What does “no tailpipe emissions” mean?
3. What preserves the learning goal without a motorized kit?
