Teacher Resources and Assessment

Use these guides to move from a transportation example to a standards-connected learning experience.

Guided educational video

Clean Energy 101: Electric Vehicles — U.S. Department of Energy (YouTube)

Recommended for Units 2 and 6. Before watching: What does an EV need to operate? During: identify energy source, storage, and motion. After: identify one benefit and one limitation for your community. The video discusses U.S. policy and cost examples; these are context-dependent and should not be treated as universal or current.

Accessibility: YouTube captions and availability should be checked by the instructor. If video access is unavailable, participants can read a short description of energy storage, electric motors, and charging, then complete the same prompts.

Lesson adaptation worksheet

  1. Identify grade, subject, duration, and learner context.
  2. Write one observable learning objective.
  3. Describe the local transportation problem and why it matters.
  4. List criteria, constraints, materials, and an unplugged alternative.
  5. Write instructions for introduction, investigation, analysis, improvement, and reflection.
  6. Specify student evidence: diagram, table, graph, model, argument, or prototype.
  7. Identify a suggested standard and verify the actual evidence it requires.
  8. Plan at least two accessibility supports and a feedback opportunity.

Final project rubric

Score each criterion 1–4 (maximum 24). A score of 4 is strong, 3 proficient, 2 developing, and 1 beginning. Apply the same rubric to the design and implementation pathways.

Standards and evidence note

NGSS codes are suggested middle-school connections; a lesson meets a performance expectation only when learners produce the required kind of evidence. Alabama 2023 Course of Study alignment should be verified by subject and grade using the official state documents. ASTA is a professional association, not an academic standards code.
