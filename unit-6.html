Unit 6: Electric Mobility and Charging Communities

Compare charging locations using access, demand, and community needs.

Duration
Four hours across two weeks
Materials
Paper and pencil; browser optional
Evidence
Site recommendation with evidence table, missing-data questions, and equity reflection.
Unit sequenceObjectives1 Learn2 Experience3 Adapt4 Apply5 ReflectKnowledge check

Learning objectives

  1. Explain the role of batteries and charging infrastructure in electric mobility.
  2. Interpret a simplified map or table of charging access.
  3. Compare proposed locations using explicit criteria and limitations.
  4. Develop a locally relevant and equitable learning investigation.

Step 1 · Learn

Understand the concept

Electric vehicles store energy in rechargeable batteries. Charging infrastructure varies by location, availability, cost, and access. An equitable plan considers people who cannot charge at home, different travel needs, grid constraints, and community consultation.

Guiding question: Compare charging locations using access, demand, and community needs.

Estimated learning time: 35 minutes. Read the explanation, create a brief concept sketch, and identify one transportation example in your community.

Step 2 · Experience

Model investigation: Who can access electric mobility?

Allow 60–75 minutes. Work with a partner where possible. Record your reasoning, not just the final answer.

  1. Use this fictional dataset: Site A (school): 200 daily visitors, 1 km from bus stop, public access 8 hours; Site B (market): 350 visitors, 0.2 km from bus stop, public access 12 hours; Site C (depot): 120 visitors, 2 km from bus stop, public access 24 hours.
  2. Define at least three criteria for comparing sites (for example transit access, opening hours, visitor needs).
  3. Rank each site using your criteria; show calculations or qualitative reasoning.
  4. Discuss whose needs may be missing from the dataset and what additional evidence you need.
  5. Choose a site provisionally, explaining trade-offs and grid feasibility questions.
  6. Propose one low-cost or unplugged way to teach this investigation.

Evidence to keep: Site recommendation with evidence table, missing-data questions, and equity reflection.

Technology and unplugged pathways

With technology: Use a shared document, spreadsheet, digital diagram, or classroom presentation to collect and explain evidence. Without technology: Use printed scenario cards, graph paper, drawings, and group discussion to demonstrate the same objective.

Accessibility: Provide written and spoken directions, readable diagrams with text labels, flexible response formats, and group roles. Do not rely only on color or on personal student disclosures.

Standards connection

MS-ETS1-1; MS-ETS1-2 (engineering design connections); MS-PS3-5 (energy-transfer conceptual connection only). These are suggested connections; check the exact performance expectation and your assessment evidence before claiming full standards alignment.

Step 4 · Apply

Classroom transfer plan

  1. Identify the target grade and subject, and state one measurable learning objective.
  2. Choose a local transportation problem and activate prior knowledge with a prediction.
  3. Model the investigation and specify what students record or produce.
  4. Ask learners to interpret the evidence, compare alternatives, and explain a decision.
  5. Add a low-cost pathway and at least two accessibility supports.
  6. Define an exit ticket or performance task that directly measures the objective.

Submission: A one- to two-page classroom adaptation plan and the evidence from your investigation. Submit through your course LMS or the method provided by the instructor; this public website does not transmit assignments.

Step 5 · Reflect

Reflection and revision

Local browser storage only; do not include identifiable student information.

Knowledge check

Choose an answer to each question and check your understanding. This self-check is not submitted to an instructor.

1. Is the busiest site automatically the best?
2. What is one missing piece of evidence?
