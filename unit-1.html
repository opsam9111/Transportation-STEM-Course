Weeks 1 and 2

Transportation Systems Communities and Careers

Transportation is more than vehicles. It is a connected system of people, infrastructure, information, energy, rules, and decisions that helps communities reach opportunities and move goods.

Estimated time
Four hours across two weeks
Required technology
Browser; printable alternative
Major evidence
Community system map
Unit sequenceObjectives1 Learn2 Experience3 Adapt4 Apply5 ReflectKnowledge checkSources

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain transportation as an interconnected system.
  2. Analyze how one system change affects people and other components.
  3. Identify careers connected with different school subjects.
  4. Create a community transportation system map.
  5. Plan one age-appropriate classroom connection.

Step 1

Learn the Concept

Reliable, affordable, and safe transportation connects people with education, work, healthcare, goods, recreation, and community life. A transportation system contains interacting parts:

People and purposesWho or what must move, and why?
Modes and vehiclesWalking, bicycles, buses, cars, rail, air, water, and freight.
InfrastructureSidewalks, roads, bridges, terminals, ports, and charging stations.
Information and controlSigns, signals, maps, sensors, schedules, data, and rules.
Energy and environmentFuel, electricity, batteries, sunlight, weather, emissions, and land.
Access and outcomesSafety, time, cost, reliability, accessibility, and opportunity.
Systems-thinking question: If a community adds electric buses without accessible stops, reliable schedules, trained technicians, or charging infrastructure, which parts remain incomplete?

Step 2

Experience a Community Journey

Select a journey to reveal the connected components that make it possible.

Use a familiar trip rather than assuming that every participant owns a car. Map its people, modes, infrastructure, information, energy, rules, strengths, and barriers. Explain who experiences a barrier most strongly and propose a feasible improvement.

Step 3

Adapt for Different Contexts

Technology

Create a digital map with routes, barriers, and proposed improvements.

Low cost

Use a printed map, paper, sticky notes, string, and symbols.

Unplugged

Build a journey map from memory or interview a community member.

Career connections

Transportation draws on engineering, planning, data analysis, safety, accessibility, logistics, environmental science, software, communication, maintenance, and policy. Connect each role with mathematics, science, computing, social studies, language arts, or the arts.

Stage 6 · Teaching and assessment guide

Make the investigation classroom-ready

Teacher implementation sequence

  1. Introduce (5–10 minutes): Present a real community transportation situation and ask students to make an initial prediction.
  2. Define (10 minutes): State the challenge, criteria, constraints, and the evidence students will collect.
  3. Investigate (20–30 minutes): Complete the transportation system mapping in pairs; record observations in a labeled table or diagram.
  4. Analyze (15 minutes): Compare results, discuss alternative explanations, and identify limitations of the model.
  5. Improve (15 minutes): Revise a decision or design using evidence, and explain the expected benefit.
  6. Assess (5–10 minutes): Collect an exit ticket that includes a claim, supporting evidence, and one remaining question.

Evidence of learning

Define a local mobility problem, identify users, infrastructure, information, and two constraints.

Subject and grade adaptations

Grade 6 social studies: annotate a neighborhood map and describe who can reach a school or clinic. Grade 7 mathematics: compare travel times using a table. Grade 8 ELA: write an evidence-based recommendation to improve a route.

Standards evidence check

Suggested NGSS connection: MS-ETS1-1. This is a planning connection, not an assertion of complete alignment. Check that the actual student task and rubric assess the expectation. Alabama course-of-study codes must be confirmed against the subject and grade before adding a specific code.

Open teacher resources, video prompts, and the final-project rubric.

Step 4

Apply to Your Teaching

Create a one-page system map for a community journey. Include at least five system components, two relationships, one barrier, one career, one improvement, and evidence that could show whether the improvement worked.

Success criteria: Show relationships rather than only a list; use a real context; recognize access and learner variability; justify the proposed improvement.

Classroom transfer: step-by-step teaching guide

Suggested 35–50 minute lesson; adapt materials, pacing, and evidence requirements to your learners.

Map a school journey

  1. Choose a journey to school, a clinic, or a market and identify users, vehicles, routes, signs, and barriers.
  2. Sketch a systems map with at least five components and arrows showing their relationships.
  3. Identify one disruption such as rain, road closure, or inaccessible crossing. Predict who is affected.
  4. Revise the map to propose one improvement; explain trade-offs in cost, safety, and accessibility.
  5. Collect an exit ticket: one system connection, one barrier, and one justified improvement.

Examples across subjects and grades

GradeSubjectClassroom application
6MathematicsRecord travel times for three routes; calculate and compare mean times.
7Social studiesCompare access to a school or clinic in two communities using a map.
8Science / technologyBuild a cause-and-effect model of a transport system failure.

Suggested NGSS connections: MS-ETS1-1; MS-ETS1-2 (engineering design connections). Verify the precise expectation and the evidence elicited before claiming full alignment.

Materials: paper, pencil, and a local transport example; use the interactive model or physical equipment only when available. Assessment: evaluate the learner’s recorded evidence, explanation, and proposed improvement.
